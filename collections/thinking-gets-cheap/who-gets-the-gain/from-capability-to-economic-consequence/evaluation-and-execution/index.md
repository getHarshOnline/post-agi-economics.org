# What makes work executable?

When a job has a measurable objective, a reliable way to judge the result and tools that can act, what still requires its executor to be human? The Agent Execution Hypothesis proposes that an artificial agent can, in principle, match or exceed human execution under the same inputs, tools and constraints. It is a claim to test, not a result obtained merely by specifying the task.

The economic stakes follow from that claim. If an agent can deliver the required quality at a lower complete cost, a firm has a reason to substitute it for some human work. If checking, integration or handling exceptions consumes the saving, cheaper model output need not make the job cheaper. And if substitution removes a worker’s income, the agent does not become the customer that worker was. Execution, its complementary work and the buyer belong in the same inquiry.

<a id="a-signal-does-not-choose-its-recipient"></a>

## A signal does not choose its recipient

Choosing what to optimise is different from executing well against that objective. Hayek’s account of prices explains how people can coordinate using knowledge dispersed among them, without each knowing every circumstance behind a price.<sup><a id="fnref-note-686179656b"></a>[1](#fn-note-686179656b)</sup> The extension in [One Yardstick, One Clock](https://post-agi-economics.org/blog/one-yardstick-one-clock/) is that a person or a machine could respond to a useful signal if it can interpret it and has the means to act. The signal’s usefulness does not by itself establish either executor’s competence.

An evaluator makes that proposal concrete for a task. It must distinguish the outcome we value from a convenient proxy. Tacit skill does not disappear because we cannot easily score it: if its contribution cannot be captured well enough for the comparison, the execution claim has not yet been made testable there.

<a id="follow-the-objective-into-an-invoice"></a>

## Follow the objective into an invoice

Pay the right supplier, the right amount, at the right time, without duplicates. The objective fits in one sentence. Following it into an invoice workflow reveals a much larger task: an ambiguous supplier, a changed bank instruction, conflicting documents and an approval still needed before money moves.

Give both executors the same process to carry through, including the moments when they need help. The evaluator can then judge what they did and what they handed over for review.

**Important**

**The comparison ends before money moves.** The task is to prepare a payment proposal or collect the records needed to review an unresolved discrepancy.

| Part of the comparison | Illustrative specification |
| --- | --- |
| Cases | Routine invoices, duplicate submissions, disputed quantities and changed bank instructions; report each category separately |
| Information | The same invoice, purchase order, receipt and dated supplier record for both executors |
| Allowed actions | Read those records, prepare a proposal, or escalate an unresolved discrepancy |
| Acceptance | Supplier, amount and supporting records agree; every unresolved instruction change is escalated |
| Consequential error | A proposal treated as ready despite an unresolved bank instruction, duplicate or unsupported amount |
| Whole cost | Preparation and review time, tool use, retries and the work required to resolve escalations |

A record saying “supplier instruction changed” can justify asking for confirmation without identifying which instruction is genuine. Correct escalation is then a useful result. A resolved test case and an unresolved live case must not be scored as if they supplied the same information.

<a id="the-objective-meets-the-documents"></a>

## The objective meets the documents

In this hypothetical workflow, a test can supply answers that the real process has to discover. If an evaluator learns which bank instruction was valid only after a payment, its feedback can classify the loss but cannot prevent it. Delayed consequences, partial information and a shifting case mix therefore belong inside the test. A system that handles ordinary invoices well may still need a different procedure for a rare, consequential exception. Abstention and escalation can be part of competent execution. The comparison should account for the time and work they require, rather than score every request for help as a failure or treat that help as free.

<a id="the-same-task-under-the-same-conditions"></a>

## The same task under the same conditions

Access changes what an executor can do. A human able to call a colleague has a different means of resolving an ambiguity from an agent limited to the supplied documents. An agent with additional records may perform better without having demonstrated superiority under identical inputs. A fair comparison specifies the task distribution, information, tools, time budget and permitted escalation. It also specifies which errors matter. Average accuracy can obscure a rare mistake whose loss dominates the value of many correctly handled cases.

The configuration is part of the comparison. Recommending a payment, preparing it and releasing it involve different effects and responsibilities. An assistant can create value by improving preparation while a person retains approval. Autonomous execution requires its own performance and authority conditions.

<a id="from-a-cheap-draft-to-completed-work"></a>

## From a cheap draft to completed work

The full cost includes checking, retries, monitoring, integration, incident handling, human escalation and delay. Some costs are fixed and spread over more cases; others grow with complexity or consequence. The baseline is the existing process with its actual overhead and errors, not an imaginary flawless human. If preparation becomes faster but review takes longer, the net saving may be small. If routine work becomes easier and people can handle exceptions better, the gain may come through a changed allocation of work rather than replacement. What matters is accepted work at the required quality and risk. [Compare complete execution](https://post-agi-economics.org/collections/thinking-gets-cheap/who-gets-the-gain/compare-complete-execution/) works through the preparation, review and correction costs for a hundred invoices.

Authorisation determines which of those effects an actor may attempt. It is separate from measured performance. The [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) addresses risk across development and use, making it a useful reference for this deployment boundary rather than a certification of any particular executor.

<a id="an-execution-claim-with-something-to-compare"></a>

## An execution claim with something to compare

A local claim can say that, for this workflow and case distribution, a configuration reaches the required outcome at a stated cost and risk. Matched performance and deployment evidence can then support or contradict it. A universal claim about all instrumentable workflows would need substantially more: a successful example cannot establish every case, and an imagined better-trained executor cannot indefinitely stand in for a specified one.

The yardstick earns its place by showing where capability works, where assistance is preferable and where the process still needs another means of acting. [The organisation around the tool](https://post-agi-economics.org/collections/thinking-gets-cheap/who-gets-the-gain/from-capability-to-economic-consequence/organisational-complements/) then determines how that local performance becomes productive use.

Follow the saving one step further. Does it lower the customer’s price, increase someone’s income or accrue to the owner of a scarce complement? A person doing less invoice work might take on other work, share in the gain or lose income. Those are different paths to [household purchasing power](https://post-agi-economics.org/collections/thinking-gets-cheap/who-gets-the-gain/from-capability-to-economic-consequence/productivity-and-purchasing-power/). The invoice comparison can test execution and its cost. Understanding the economy it changes requires following both the work that remains and the income that reaches its buyers.
