From capability to economic consequence

Follow a capability through deployed work, production and labour to what reaches households.

An attractive result from one task can tempt us to jump straight to the future of work. The intervening questions determine whether the result travels: can it become dependable work in an organisation, what response does that work produce, and whose income or spending changes?

The order begins with an execution comparison so that the supposed gain has a definite meaning. It then follows the surrounding organisation before examining labour, household resources and institutional arrangements. A later conclusion can depend on an earlier link being established, but it cannot be read off it. That is why a purchase response can be a real benefit while its labour and distributional consequences remain worth investigating.

Explore this sequence

  1. What makes work executable? An execution hypothesis connects measurable work to automation, its full cost and the buyers who depend on the income it replaces.
  2. The work around the tool Co-investment, process redesign and learning determine how a local capability becomes production.
  3. The changing work behind an occupation Task exposure, workplace assistance and early-career hiring reveal different parts of how AI reaches workers.
  4. Who gets to buy what machines produce? Cheaper services can coexist with a household unable to afford them. Follow prices, lost wages and other income through its budget.
  5. Same technology, different economy One cost saving can reach customers, a supplier, worker-owners or a public budget. Ownership and the rules around it decide which.
