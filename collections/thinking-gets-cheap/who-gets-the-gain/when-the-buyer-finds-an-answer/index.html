When the buyer finds an answer

A buyer hesitates because a product description leaves a question unresolved. An answer can change whether the purchase happens. In that familiar situation, the useful service is more than a cheaper way to process a transaction already certain to occur. It changes the possibility of the transaction itself.

This gives AI deployment an economic path different from saving minutes on an unchanged task. The distinction matters because sales, operating cost and the work needed to fulfil an order can move together. We need to follow that movement, rather than select a labour-saving story before observing what changed.

Seven retail experiments on whether buyers purchase more

The demand side need not remain a hypothetical destination for a production gain. Fang and coauthors' June 2026 paper examines seven randomised GenAI workflow experiments on one cross-border retail platform in 2023–24. They span service, matching, advertising and seller assistance. Sales effects range from no detectable effect to 16.3 percent against the workflow's existing practice. The gains operate mainly through more people buying, rather than bigger carts; returns and ratings do not deteriorate in the reported comparisons.

Annualising the four positive sales effects gives an estimated USD 4.6–5.2 in additional sales per consumer, assuming linear addition without workflow overlap or synergies. It is a calculation from short experimental periods, not a year of observed extra sales. The study does not observe the full capital, engineering, inference and organisational costs, so the sales estimate is not net return. These details leave a constructive result intact: particular deployed workflows changed purchases in a randomised comparison.

A transaction becomes possible

The mechanism has a different economic shape from an assistant completing the same support case faster. If a buyer finds a suitable item or gets an answer needed to decide, work can improve by resolving a transaction that otherwise would not happen. Demand and the useful service change together. The firm's next question is whether the extra transactions repay the deployment and change its need for support, fulfilment or other work. A labour-saving story is only one possible account.

The experiments do not isolate that explanation from persuasion, attention, trust or novelty. More purchases could follow clearer information, more compelling presentation or some combination of them. The difference matters to the product: a buyer finding a better match and a buyer being persuaded to act can produce the same immediate sales result while leaving different reasons to return. Conversion locates the observed change; understanding the service requires following the buyer beyond it.

The retailer and the economy

For the retailer, resolving that hesitation can create an additional sale. For the buyer, it might replace a purchase elsewhere or bring a future purchase forward. Lower search friction can improve the choice without increasing the buyer's total budget. Following the gain beyond the platform requires those alternatives to be observed, together with the additional work, hours, pay and income claims that the transactions create. The experiment establishes a local purchase response; tracing its wider destination is a further economic question.

A local purchase response is worth understanding in its own right. It can reveal an unresolved need that better information helps a buyer meet. Whether the response creates a lasting return depends on the complete operating process, including what additional transactions require. Whether it changes the buyer's room to live depends on income and prices beyond that purchase. Finding an answer can improve a transaction without answering every question about its economic destination.
