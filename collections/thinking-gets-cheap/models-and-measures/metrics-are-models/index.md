# The economy inside the measure

A service answers twice as many questions at half the price. Its customers spend the same total amount. Has the economy gained a larger service, merely produced more text, or moved effort into correcting the answers?

In this hypothetical comparison, expenditure counts payments, answer counts track output, and useful service asks whether the answer helped the person who requested it. As AI changes production, an increase in one measure can come from a change in the work or from a change in which parts of the work are counted.

<a id="producing-ai-and-producing-with-it"></a>

## Producing AI and producing with it

A supplier builds software, a factory uses it and a customer buys the factory's output. The software is an input to the factory's production. Adding the gross sales at every layer would count some of the same activity more than once. The [Bureau of Economic Analysis' January 2025 study](https://www.bea.gov/research/papers/2025/concepts-and-challenges-measuring-production-artificial-intelligence-us) distinguishes AI production from its uses within national accounting. It considers a thematic satellite account spanning manufacturing, software, data services and research, with classification and data limitations. Locating the technology in an existing accounting system is substantive work: the categories determine where its contribution appears, rather than making GDP meaningless simply because the technology is new.

Here is a deliberately stripped-down supply chain, with no imports or other intermediate purchases:

| Producer | Output sold | Intermediate purchases | Value added |
| --- | --: | --: | --: |
| Software supplier | 20 | 0 | 20 |
| Factory buying that software | 100 | 20 | 80 |
| Combined | 120 in gross sales | 20 | 100 |

The customer buys the factory's final output for 100. Adding 120 in gross sales would count the software once as the supplier's output and again inside the factory's price. Subtracting intermediate use gives 100 of value added. In this example, both the cost and the supplier's output concern software consumed in the period; a capitalised asset would require a different accounting treatment. The example explains the boundary, not a proposed AI satellite account.

<a id="quantity-quality-and-welfare"></a>

## Quantity, quality and welfare

Return to the service that produces twice as many answers at half the price. Nominal expenditure can remain unchanged. If the answers are equally useful, more service is available; if many need correction, an answer count overstates useful output. New users gaining access introduce a welfare question extending beyond the expenditure total. Quality adjustment depends on purpose. Accuracy, timeliness, accessibility and consequential errors measure different aspects of a service. A price index cannot settle their social value by quietly assigning them one weight. Likewise, a free output may create value, impose costs, replace a paid service or consume attention. Accounting and welfare require connected arguments with different boundaries.

<a id="operations-jobs-and-outcomes"></a>

## Operations, jobs and outcomes

Compute utilisation compares performed operations with a capacity benchmark. A completed-job measure follows a workflow; revenue follows transactions; an outcome measure follows the result someone needs. Their relationships depend on the workload and the process between them. A provider could raise utilisation by accepting less valuable work. It could reduce average job cost while making important jobs wait longer. A team could produce more drafts while finishing fewer accepted documents. In each case the convenient number improves while another consequence deteriorates. A useful comparison therefore carries the workload mix, denominator, target and relevant constraints with it. Where quality changes, the comparison needs to show that change rather than silently treating every unit as equivalent.

<a id="the-incentive-inside-the-observation"></a>

## The incentive inside the observation

When funding or permission depends on a metric, participants respond to it. A delivered-capacity measure can encourage completion while neglecting whom the service reaches. A count of drafts can encourage drafting at the expense of review. Sometimes that response is the intended incentive; sometimes it moves work away from the purpose the measure was selected to serve. Additional measures can make a trade-off visible, but they also cost effort to collect. Combining them into a single index introduces a choice about their relative importance. The observation rule is consequently part of the institutional arrangement, not a neutral window onto it.

A repair might change the denominator, preserve a separate quality boundary, follow a linked outcome or decline to aggregate. Broadening [the event counted as AI adoption](https://post-agi-economics.org/collections/thinking-gets-cheap/models-and-measures/measuring-ai-diffusion/) can raise a reported rate without changing a single firm's activity. Repairing that comparison requires a common counted event or a visible break in the series. A [changed production relation](https://post-agi-economics.org/collections/thinking-gets-cheap/from-abundance-to-a-changed-description/transition-dynamics/) poses a different problem: the process itself has changed, not just the way it was counted.

The measure remains useful when its interpretation can travel with the number: what activity it counts, for which question, under which definition. That is how an account can evolve with the work without losing the consequence it was meant to reveal.
