Models and measures

What a count of AI use actually counts, and how a forecast is checked against the event it named.

How much AI is in the economy? A count of firms using a tool and a count of completed autonomous tasks give different answers because they count different things. Money spent on software asks another question again.

The economic question gives a counted event its meaning. A count which once located a new activity can become uninformative when that activity spreads into tasks the survey never distinguished. A revised definition can then improve the observation while breaking comparison with earlier years. Understanding that trade-off matters before the series becomes evidence about production, or an input to a clock.

The definition also matters when a forecast meets its outcome. If a prediction concerns recurring use but the later survey counts any trial, a rise in the number does not settle the prediction. Across enough comparable cases, with each prediction’s original event and information preserved, we can ask whether the model separates cases with different chances of success, and whether outcomes occur about as often as it predicts. A forecast has something to answer to when we can tell what happened without changing what it said.
