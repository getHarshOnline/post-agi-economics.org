Author: Harsh Joshi

When machines can do more of the work, what changes for the people living in the economy?

Post-AGI Economics studies how cheaper machine agency changes production, scarcity, ownership and income. More capability can open new activities while making power, infrastructure or access more valuable. The gains depend on how firms, households and institutions adapt, and on who can participate.

 

<a id="where-the-constraint-moves"></a>

## Where the constraint moves

A task can become feasible before a firm can deploy it. Deployment may expose a shortage of power, infrastructure or another input. Investment can relieve that shortage while creating another source of scarcity.

[Follow the constraints](https://post-agi-economics.org/collections/thinking-gets-cheap/moving-constraints/)

 

<a id="who-gets-the-gain"></a>

## Who gets the gain

A production gain does not tell us who can afford the result. Prices, wages, ownership and public arrangements carry that gain into household resources in different ways.

[Follow the gain](https://post-agi-economics.org/collections/thinking-gets-cheap/who-gets-the-gain/)

 

<a id="models-and-measures"></a>

## Models and measures

Sometimes a familiar model needs new values. Sometimes another decision-maker or relationship has entered the activity. More detail earns its place when it restores a connection that changes the answer.

[Examine the models](https://post-agi-economics.org/collections/thinking-gets-cheap/models-and-measures/)

A model can be right about its totals and still miss the relation needed for a decision.

An economy can produce more while some households lose the income with which they bought its output. Firms can become more productive while a scarce input captures much of the gain. Following those relationships requires a model that represents the actors, constraints and channels relevant to the decision.

That question connects the programme to [MONAD’s study of changing descriptions](https://causality.in/monad/): when does a model need new values, and when must it make room for a different account of the activity?

Ask what changes the answer.

A workshop can gain many more plans while its machine time still limits production. In that case, the familiar relation can survive while the bottleneck moves. Two purchasing agents can spend the same amount yet select parts that no longer fit together. For that question, the account needs to retain who chose which part.

These [worked comparisons](https://post-agi-economics.org/collections/thinking-gets-cheap/from-abundance-to-a-changed-description/transition-dynamics/) give the programme a practical test for richer models. Firm and household studies then ask which mechanisms occur outside the construction. [Models and measures](https://post-agi-economics.org/collections/thinking-gets-cheap/models-and-measures/) keeps that observation question beside the economic one.

How much time does an economy have to adapt?

[One Yardstick](https://post-agi-economics.org/one-yardstick/) connects the evaluation of agent execution to a proposed transition clock: how much time might an economy have to adapt, and what kind of economy would it become? That living thesis develops alongside the inquiry into moving constraints and rents. A capability becoming accessible, a firm changing its process and a household losing income are different transitions. Understanding their relationship is part of the work needed to connect a clock to the economy it describes.
