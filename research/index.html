Questions about the research

What does this research ask?

Post-AGI Economics asks what changes in an economy, and in the models that describe it, when cognitive work gets cheap. The main line of inquiry follows production, scarcity and who receives the gain.

Does this inquiry depend on predicting one AGI date?

No. Capability, affordable access, reliable operation and institutional change move at different speeds. The inquiry follows particular frontiers and the ways they affect one another. A machine does not need to cross one universal threshold for its economic consequences to matter.

When does the economic model need to change?

A model that represents plans and machine time can still work when machinery becomes the limit. New decision makers, ownership arrangements or relationships may require a different account. The question is which consequence the model must preserve. The worked comparisons show when to recalculate within a model and when to retain something it previously left out.

Why follow ownership and income alongside productivity?

Producing more does not determine who receives the gain. Prices, wages, ownership claims and public arrangements connect a production improvement to the resources of particular households. Those channels also affect demand and the next production decision.

How does One Yardstick fit the programme?

One Yardstick begins with a fair comparison of human and agent execution on a defined task, then asks what recurring economic use would require. The wider programme follows adoption, moving constraints and distribution across many such changes. One Yardstick’s proposed transition clock asks how much time an economy has to adapt, and the programme’s worked examples compare what follows under stated conditions.
